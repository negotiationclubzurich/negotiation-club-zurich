Negotiation education beyond the classroom

Negotiation is a fundamental skill in academic life and in almost every professional environment. Students negotiate in group projects, internships, job interviews, salary discussions and future business relationships.

Negotiation Club Zurich creates opportunities to practise these skills in a structured, supportive and interactive environment. Participants work through realistic scenarios, experiment with different approaches and learn from feedback.

We welcome conversations with universities, academic departments and student organisations interested in making practical negotiation learning more accessible.

Why negotiation skills matter for students

Academic knowledge is important, but students also benefit from learning how to communicate interests, manage disagreement and reach constructive agreements.

Professional preparation

Develop confidence for job interviews, salary conversations, internships and future workplace discussions.

Communication and collaboration

Practise listening, asking effective questions, understanding different perspectives and managing conflicting interests.

Practical problem-solving

Learn to identify interests, generate options and work towards agreements that create value for different parties.

Possible university workshop formats

We are open to discussing different formats depending on the objectives, audience and available time of your institution.

Introduction to negotiation

An accessible introduction to negotiation principles, preparation and communication. Suitable for students with little or no previous experience.

Interactive negotiation simulations

Participants take on different roles in realistic negotiation cases and practise applying concepts in small groups.

Focused skills workshops

Sessions exploring topics such as BATNA, anchoring, interests, creating value, persuasion and reaching agreements.

Student community events

Joint events and activities that bring together students interested in negotiation, communication and professional development.

Topics students can practise

Depending on the workshop objectives, exercises can explore a range of practical negotiation concepts.

  • Preparation and strategy Clarifying objectives, priorities and alternatives before a negotiation
  • BATNA and ZOPA Understanding alternatives and possible agreement zones
  • Anchoring and offers Making and responding to proposals effectively
  • Interests and value creation Moving beyond positions to explore mutual gains
  • Communication and active listening Asking questions, understanding perspectives and building trust
  • Closing agreements Evaluating options and reaching clear, workable outcomes

How a workshop can work

Our practical learning approach combines short theoretical inputs with active participation and structured reflection.

  • 1. Introduction Introduce the negotiation topic and key concepts.
  • 2. Preparation Participants receive a case and prepare their negotiation strategy.
  • 3. Simulation Participants negotiate in pairs or small groups.
  • 4. Debrief Discuss approaches, outcomes and lessons learned.
  • 5. Reflection Identify techniques participants can apply in everyday situations.

Frequently asked questions

Who are university negotiation workshops for?

They can be designed for university students, student associations and academic communities interested in developing practical negotiation and communication skills. Previous experience is not required for introductory activities.

Do participants need previous negotiation experience?

No. Introductory formats can be designed for beginners. More focused sessions can also explore specific negotiation techniques and frameworks.

Can workshops be adapted to our university?

We are open to discussing the learning objectives, target audience and format with each interested institution.

Can student organisations organise joint events?

Yes. We welcome enquiries from student associations and initiatives interested in exploring joint events and negotiation-related activities.

Are the activities conducted in English?

English is the working language of Negotiation Club Zurich.
